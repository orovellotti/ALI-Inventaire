---
name: IUCN spatial priority
description: Required spatial hierarchy for IUCN statuses, including the stricter rule for flora.
---

Use IUCN Red List assessments in this strict order: regional, national, European, then global only as secondary context or a cautious fallback for non-plant groups.

For flora, a global-only IUCN status must never determine the conservation level or be presented as the sufficient status. If no regional, national, or European assessment is available, show the territorial status as needing confirmation.

**Why:** impact studies concern a limited territory, and local assessments are more spatially relevant than a global assessment. The user explicitly requires a stricter treatment for plants because global flora assessments may not reflect French or regional field knowledge.

**How to apply:** enforce the hierarchy consistently in scoring, species cards, reports, and exports. Never let legacy global-derived heuristics create a strong plant issue when territorial enrichment is missing. A potential species may rise as an “enjeu documentaire potentiel élevé” when local compatibility is sufficient and a regional CR/EN/VU status is supplied, but it must remain separate from occurrences and require field confirmation.
